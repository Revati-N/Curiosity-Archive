Kerosene is a flammable liquid hydrocarbon fuel derived from petroleum. It sits in the middle of the distillation range — heavier than gasoline, lighter than diesel.

# How it's made:

Crude oil is fed into a distillation column and heated. Different fractions separate out at different boiling points — kerosene comes off between roughly 150–275°C. This straight-run kerosene is then hydrotreated (exposed to hydrogen under pressure) to remove sulfur and other impurities, which improves combustion quality and reduces emissions. Some refineries do additional processing like merox treating to further clean it up.

The result is a clear to pale yellow liquid used as jet fuel (Jet-A is essentially refined kerosene), in heating, as a cooking fuel, and in lanterns.
