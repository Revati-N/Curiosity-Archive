Kerosene is a flammable liquid hydrocarbon fuel derived from petroleum. It sits in the middle of the distillation range — heavier than gasoline, lighter than diesel.

# How it's made:

Crude oil is fed into a distillation column and heated. Different fractions separate out at different boiling points — kerosene comes off between roughly 150–275°C. This straight-run kerosene is then hydrotreated (exposed to hydrogen under pressure) to remove sulfur and other impurities, which improves combustion quality and reduces emissions. Some refineries do additional processing like merox treating to further clean it up.

The result is a clear to pale yellow liquid used as jet fuel (Jet-A is essentially refined kerosene), in heating, as a cooking fuel, and in lanterns.

**Types of Kerosene**:
- Jet-A / Jet-A1 — aviation grade, very pure, low freezing point
- K-1 — standard home heating and lamp grade
- K-2 — lower purity, used in some heaters and equipment

**Properties**:
- Boiling range: 150–275°C
- Flash point: ~38–72°C (flammable but less volatile than gasoline, which is why it's safer to store)
- Energy density: ~35 MJ/L
- Clear to pale yellow in color, distinctive smell from trace aromatics

**Uses**:
- Jet fuel (largest global use by volume)
- Cooking and heating in developing countries
- Tractor fuel (older engines)
- Solvent and cleaning agent in industry
- Rocket propellant (RP-1 is a highly refined kerosene — used in SpaceX Falcon 9 first stages)

**Fun fact**: RP-1 kerosene + liquid oxygen is one of the most common rocket propellant combinations. It's what lifted the Saturn V off the ground in the first stage.
