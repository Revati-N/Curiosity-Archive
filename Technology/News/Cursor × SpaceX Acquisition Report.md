## Overview

On **August 14, 2026**, SpaceX completed its $60 billion all-stock acquisition of Anysphere,
the San Francisco-based startup behind the AI coding tool **Cursor** — making it the largest
acquisition of a venture-backed startup ever recorded. The deal was formally announced on
June 16, 2026, days after SpaceX's record-breaking Nasdaq IPO, and closed ahead of its
originally expected Q3 2026 timeline. Cursor now operates as a wholly owned subsidiary
of SpaceX.

---

## Background

Founded in 2022, Cursor is an AI-first code editor built on VS Code that integrates large
language models into the software development workflow — offering AI-powered code completion,
natural language editing, codebase chat, and autonomous code generation. The company grew
rapidly, crossing $1 billion in annualized revenue in late 2025 and reaching approximately
$4 billion ARR by mid-2026 — one of the fastest SaaS ramps in enterprise software history.

## The Acquisition

| Detail | Info |
|---|---|
| **Acquirer** | SpaceX (Space Exploration Technologies) |
| **Target** | Anysphere (Cursor) |
| **Deal Value** | $60 billion (all-stock, Class A shares) |
| **Announced** | June 16, 2026 |
| **Closed** | August 14, 2026 |
| **Structure** | Cursor becomes wholly owned SpaceX subsidiary |

The groundwork was laid in **April 2026**, when SpaceX announced a partnership with Cursor
on model training — securing an option to acquire the company for $60B or pay $10B for
their collaborative work. SpaceX exercised the acquisition option two trading days after
its IPO debut. The deal represented a 3.4% dilution at SpaceX's IPO valuation.

The strategic rationale is vertical integration: SpaceX is assembling an AI stack spanning
model development (via its SpaceXAI division, formerly xAI), coding tooling (Cursor), and
raw compute infrastructure (Colossus supercomputer in Memphis, Tennessee).

---

## Model Advancements

Cursor's model lineup, called **Composer**, has seen rapid iteration since the partnership began:

- **Composer (original)** — Introduced as an agentic coding system.
- **Composer 1.5** — Expanded reinforcement learning by over 20×.
- **Composer 2** — Added continued pretraining, achieving frontier-level performance at
  substantially lower cost than competing models.
- **Composer 2.5** — Built on Moonshot's Kimi K2.5 base; trained with 25× more synthetic
  tasks than Composer 2.
- **Composer 3** *(announced, not yet released)* — A 1.5-trillion parameter model trained
  from scratch on SpaceX's Colossus cluster using 10–20× more compute than Composer 1.
  Targets Claude Opus / GPT-5.5 class performance. Expected H2 2026.

The jointly developed SpaceXAI–Cursor model was reported to target performance rivaling
Anthropic's Opus 4.8 and OpenAI's GPT-5.5.

## Other Notable Developments

- **Origin** — Announced at Cursor's Compile 2026 event, Origin is a new git hosting
  platform designed from the ground up for AI-native and multi-agent workflows — positioned
  as a GitHub competitor. Ships fall 2026; waitlist open.
- **Cursor Mobile** — iOS beta launched, allowing developers to prompt agents and remotely
  control desktop sessions from their phone.
- **Gartner Recognition** — Cursor was named a Leader in the 2026 Gartner® Magic Quadrant™
  for Enterprise AI Coding Agents (May 2026).
- **Graphite acquisition** — Cursor acquired code-review startup Graphite in December 2025
  for a reported $290M+.

---

## Market Context

Despite explosive revenue growth, Cursor's market share declined from ~41% in June 2025
to ~26% by May 2026, per Ramp corporate spending data — reflecting intensifying competition
from GitHub Copilot, Windsurf, and others. The SpaceX acquisition effectively ends the era
of independent AI coding tools competing purely on product; Cursor enters a well-resourced
ecosystem with compute infrastructure its competitors cannot match.
